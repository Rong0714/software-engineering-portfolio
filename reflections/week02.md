# Task 1

Brief A: Aegis Flight-Control Software Update
Waterfall: Requirements are stable and safety risk is extremely high. The 12–18 month timeline allows a structured process 
with thorough testing, traceability, documentation, and certification. Customer involvement can occur through 
formal QA reviews.

Risk: If a requirement changes late, it can be expensive and time-consuming to modify because the process is highly sequential.

Brief B: Riverside Bakery Marketing Website
RAD:	Requirements are uncertain and the owner wants to see prototypes and give frequent feedback. The 3-week deadline and 
low technical/safety risk make rapid prototyping ideal. Little formal documentation is needed.

Risk: Frequent customer changes could cause scope creep and make it difficult to finish within the 3-week deadline.

Brief C: CampusCircle Community App
Incremental:	Requirements are expected to change based on real user feedback. The team can release a basic version quickly, 
then add features incrementally. Risk is relatively low, and users can provide feedback after each release.

Risk: Early increments may be built on incomplete requirements, which could lead to rework when user feedback changes 
the product direction.
